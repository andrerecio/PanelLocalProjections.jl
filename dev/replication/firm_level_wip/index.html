Firm-level applications (work in progress)

Almuzara & Sancibrián (2026, §5) re-estimate three firm-level studies of monetary policy with t-LAHR: Ottonello & Winberry (2020, OW20), Cloyne, Ferreira, Froemel & Surico (2023, CFFS23) and Jeenas & Lagos (2024, JL24). All three use quarterly Compustat; OW20 and JL24 also use CRSP, CFFS23 WorldScope, and OW20 Datastream incorporation dates. These data are licensed (Compustat and CRSP through WRDS; Datastream and WorldScope from LSEG/Refinitiv) and cannot be redistributed. The authors' replication packages contain their code and results but not the raw data. This page therefore maps the specifications to panellp calls for users who have those licences (requires WRDS, and LSEG for Datastream/WorldScope). None of them has been run on the data: the calls below were run only on synthetic panels of the same shape, to check that they estimate. For a panel LP-IV on public data, see the US state example. The specifications are summarised from the papers and from Almuzara & Sancibrián's Table 2; check them against the papers before use.

Almuzara & Sancibrián's common equation (eq. 16) is

\[\Delta k_{i,t+h} = a_{i,h} + d_{h,g(i)t} + \beta(h)' s_{i,t-1} X_t + \eta(h)' W_{it} + v_{h,it},\]

with firm effects, group × quarter effects, a lagged firm characteristic $s_{i,t-1}$ interacted with the monetary shock $X_t$, and controls. Their re-estimation adds $\hat p(h)$ lags of the outcome and of $s_{i,t-1}X_t$ (LagAugmented()) and reports t-LAHR intervals (TLAHR()); quarters can be integers or Dates with frequency = :quarter.

Ottonello & Winberry (2020)

Source of the specification: the replication package of Ottonello & Winberry (2020), "Financial Heterogeneity and the Investment Channel of Monetary Policy", Econometrica 88(6), 2473–2502 (the Econometrica replication package, June 2020). Its construct_panel.do builds the sample from Compustat quarterly fundamentals, the CRSP–Compustat merge, CRSP market values (for distance to default) and Datastream incorporation dates. Its results/ folder holds their estimates, the targets of a replication. The package excludes the raw data "to respect potential copyright restrictions".

  • Outcome: $\log K_{i,t+h} - \log K_{i,t-1}$, the long difference of the capital stock (ldiff(logk)).
  • Exposure: leverage or distance to default at $t - 1$, standardised.
  • Shock: high-frequency monetary-policy surprises aggregated to quarters (expansionary positive).
  • Effects: firm, and sector (8 SIC groups) × quarter.
  • Controls: lagged firm controls (sales growth, size, current assets) and the exposure interacted with lagged GDP growth.
  • Their standard errors: clustered by firm and quarter, 1990Q1–2007Q4.
f = @formula(ldiff(logk) ~ lags(dd, 1) & mp + lags(dd, 1) + lags(salesgr, 1) +
             lags(size, 1) + lags(curassets, 1) + lags(dd, 1) & lags(gdpgr, 1) +
             fe(firm) + fe(sector) & fe(quarter))
r = panellp(f, data; id = :firm, time = :quarter, frequency = :quarter, horizon = 12,
            shock = "L1.dd & mp", estimator = LagAugmented())
summarize(r, TLAHR(); level = 0.90)          # their two-way clustering, for comparison:
w = panellp(f, data; id = :firm, time = :quarter, frequency = :quarter, horizon = 12,
            shock = "L1.dd & mp")
summarize(w, Clustered(:unit, :time); level = 0.90)

The exposure varies over time, so the synthetic weights of TLAHR() are the lagged characteristic residualised within sector × quarter cells.

Jeenas & Lagos (2024), reduced form

  • Outcome: the investment rate in levels, with one lag of it as a control.
  • Exposure: lagged stock turnover, interacted with an indicator of the bottom half of the liquidity distribution ($I_{L,t-1}$). Every term of the regression is interacted with it, the fixed effects included.
  • Effects: firm, and 2-digit SIC × quarter.
  • Shock: Jarociński–Karadi surprises.
  • Their standard errors: clustered by firm and 3-digit SIC × quarter, 1990Q1–2016Q4.

With two exposures of the same shock (turnover × high and × low liquidity), TLAHR() weights each by its exposure residualised on the other (see t-LAHR). The interactions of the fixed effects with the liquidity indicator are fixed effects of the pairs, fe(firm) & fe(lowliq) and fe(sic2) & fe(quarter) & fe(lowliq); the firm × liquidity effects are nested in the firms, so they satisfy LagAugmented's requirement of unit effects. The interacted slopes are products such as lags(x, 1) & lowliq. The lag of the outcome that LagAugmented adds, L1.x, is the sum of the two interacted lags, so it is left out of the regression (silently: the formula's columns span it).

f = @formula(leads(x) ~ lags(turn, 1) & highliq & mp + lags(turn, 1) & lowliq & mp +
             lags(x, 1) & highliq + lags(x, 1) & lowliq + lags(turn, 1) & highliq +
             lags(turn, 1) & lowliq + fe(firm) & fe(lowliq) +
             fe(sic2) & fe(quarter) & fe(lowliq))
r = panellp(f, data; id = :firm, time = :quarter, frequency = :quarter, horizon = 12,
            shock = ["L1.turn & highliq & mp", "L1.turn & lowliq & mp"],
            estimator = LagAugmented())

Cloyne, Ferreira, Froemel & Surico (2023): panel LP-IV

  • Outcome: $X_{i,t+h} - X_{i,t-1}$, with X capital expenditure over beginning-of-period net PP&E (ldiff(capex)).
  • Endogenous: the change in the one-year rate, $\Delta R_t$ (end of quarter, +25 bp normalisation), interacted with four age × dividend groups at $t - 1$ (g1–g4, dummies of the lagged group).
  • Instrument: the Gertler–Karadi (2015) proxy-SVAR shock × the same groups.
  • Effects: firm and quarter-of-year (seasonal). There are no time effects, so the group responses include general-equilibrium effects. The group dummies enter as controls (three of them, as the four sum to one).
  • Their standard errors: Driscoll–Kraay with 12 lags, 1986Q1–2016Q4.
f = @formula(ldiff(capex) ~ (g1 & dR + g2 & dR + g3 & dR + g4 & dR ~
                             g1 & gk + g2 & gk + g3 & gk + g4 & gk) +
             g2 + g3 + g4 + fe(firm) + fe(qofyear))
r = panellp(f, data; id = :firm, time = :quarter, frequency = :quarter, horizon = 20)
summarize(r, DriscollKraaySE(12); term = "g1 & dR", level = 0.90)   # their bands
firststage(r; vcov = DriscollKraaySE(12))                          # KP F, 4 endogenous
# A&S's re-estimation: lag augmentation and t-LAHR (A.4, CR0 by period)
ra = panellp(f, data; id = :firm, time = :quarter, frequency = :quarter, horizon = 20,
             estimator = LagAugmented(CappedLags(4)))
summarize(ra, TLAHR(refine = false); term = "g1 & dR", level = 0.90)

With four endogenous shocks, ICLags weights the reduced form with the exposure of the first one (g1), so LagAugmented() works too. TLAHR(refine = true) is not defined for IV fits (see panel LP-IV).

Jeenas & Lagos (2024): instrumental variables

  • Endogenous: $\log q_{it}$ (Tobin's q), a firm-level variable.
  • Instruments: $\mathcal T_{i,t-1}\varepsilon^m_t$ and $I_{L,t-1}\mathcal T_{i,t-1}\varepsilon^m_t$ (the turnover × shock interactions of the reduced form).
  • Their first-stage F: 16.0 at h = 0 for the investment rate (fn. 50).
f = @formula(leads(x) ~ (logq ~ lags(turn, 1) & highliq & mp + lags(turn, 1) & lowliq & mp) +
             lags(x, 1) & highliq + lags(x, 1) & lowliq + lags(turn, 1) & highliq +
             lags(turn, 1) & lowliq + fe(firm) & fe(lowliq) +
             fe(sic2) & fe(quarter) & fe(lowliq))
r = panellp(f, data; id = :firm, time = :quarter, frequency = :quarter, horizon = 12,
            clusters = ((:sic3, :quarter),))
summarize(r, Clustered(:unit, (:sic3, :quarter)))        # their clustering
summarize(r, TLAHR(refine = false))                      # by period
firststage(r; vcov = TLAHR(refine = false))

The endogenous regressor is not an aggregate series times an exposure, so ICLags (whose weights need one) does not apply; LagAugmented(ConstantLags(p)) lags $\log q$ as an endogenous regressor, instrumented by the lags of the instruments.
