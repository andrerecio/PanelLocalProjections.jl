Ilzetzki (2024): Learning by Necessity

Ilzetzki (2024, American Economic Review) estimates the response of aircraft plants' productivity to demand in World War II by panel LP-IV: 143 production lines, monthly, 1940–1945, with the output of a line instrumented by the output of the other lines of its broad aircraft type. Two features of his regressions are general options of panellp since v0.3 (Lead paths and negative horizons):

  • pre-trends: the loop runs from a negative horizon ($h = -6$ in his Figure A.4, $-1$ elsewhere), with the outcome $y_{i,t+h} - y_{i,t-1}$ at every $h$;
  • the path of future output as controls (his "diff-in-diff" files LPIV_DiD.do and LPIV3D_DiD.do, Figure 9c–d): at $h > 0$ the leads $x_{t+1}, \dots, x_{t+h}$ of the treatment enter as exogenous regressors, so the coefficient of $x_t$ is the response to demand at $t$ holding later demand fixed.

There is no binary treatment and no clean-control sample here: it is not the LP-DiD estimator, only its outcome and the idea of conditioning on later treatment.

The data and code are the author's replication package (code BSD-3, data CC BY 4.0); they are not distributed with this package. crosscheck/stata/ilzetzki_local.do builds the data with his WWII_Planes.do, unmodified, and re-runs the regressions with ivreg2 exactly as his files issue them; test/test_ilzetzki.jl (tag :local_ei, skipped when the data are absent) compares.

Results

Against ivreg2 on his data, for Figure 9(c) and 9(d), Figures 6(a–b)/A.4 ($h = -6, \dots, 18$) and an OLS variant: coefficients and standard errors within 1e-8 relative (4e-12 absolute), the number of observations, the degrees of freedom of the bands and the first-stage F exact (the F to 5e-15). Remarks

  • The published bands use the classical variance, which assumes errors that are homoskedastic and uncorrelated within a production line. Clustered by line (Clustered(:unit)), the standard errors of Figure 9(c) are 1.4 to 2.2 times as large: the classical 95% bands exclude zero at $h = 0$ and $7$–$10$, the clustered ones at no horizon.
  • The bands mix conventions: a standard error without a small-sample factor and a Student-t quantile with $N - K$ degrees of freedom.
  • At $h = -1$ the outcome is identically zero, so the coefficient and its standard error are zero: the plotted origin. ivreg2 fails there with robust or cluster() (r(504)); panellp returns the zero with any variance.
  • The package stores variables as floats (Stata's default), so its own outputs differ from a double-precision run by about 5e-8.
  • Not reproduced here (read, not run): the files with horizon-specific controls built from long differences of other variables (growth controls, spillovers), the cumulative-output treatment (LP_MultiIV_Reg.do), and the tables.

Reference

Ilzetzki, E. (2024). Learning by necessity: Government demand, capacity constraints, and productivity growth. American Economic Review. Replication package: AEA Data and Code Repository (AEAREP-5450).
