LP-DiD

LP-DiD is the local-projections approach to difference-in-differences of Dube, Girardi, Jordà & Taylor (2025, Journal of Applied Econometrics; DGJT below). With a binary treatment $D_{it}$ that units enter at different dates, it regresses the long difference of the outcome on the treatment switch and time effects,

\[y_{i,t+h} - y_{i,t-1} = \beta_h\, \Delta D_{it} + \delta^h_t + e^h_{it}, \qquad h = -Q, \dots, H,\]

on the rows that are either newly treated ($\Delta D_{it} = 1$) or clean controls ($D_{i,t+h} = 0$). Every comparison is then between units entering treatment at $t$ and units not yet treated at $t + h$, which avoids the comparisons with already-treated units that bias two-way fixed-effects estimators when effects are heterogeneous.

r = panellp(@formula(ldiff(y) ~ firstdiff(D) + fe(t)), data;
            id = :id, time = :t, horizon = -5:8, estimator = LPDiD())
summarize(r, Clustered(:unit))        # event study: h = −5, …, 8

The formula is the regression: the response is ldiff(y), the shock is firstdiff(D) with D the 0/1 treatment column, and the time effects are written out (fe(t); an error without them). Covariates, lags of the outcome and further fixed effects are ordinary terms. Negative horizons are the pre-treatment coefficients; $h = -1$ is zero by construction (the long difference is identically zero), with a zero standard error.

The method is implemented from the paper. The Stata command lpdid (Busch & Girardi, GPL-3) served only as a reference to compare outputs with: every option below reproduces it to round-off on the cross-check panels (crosscheck/README.md, Phase 7), and the audit page lists where the two differ.

What the coefficient estimates

With $N^1_g$ units entering at $g$ and $N^0_{g,h}$ clean controls, $\hat\beta_h = \sum_g \omega_{g,h} \hat\tau_{g,h}$ (DGJT eq. 9–10), where $\hat\tau_{g,h}$ is the difference between the mean long differences of the two groups and

\[\omega_{g,h} \propto \frac{N^1_g N^0_{g,h}}{N^1_g + N^0_{g,h}} .\]

The weights are positive: a variance-weighted average effect on the treated. With reweight = true every treated row has the same weight, and $\hat\beta_h$ is the equally weighted average effect on the treated, $\sum_g N^1_g \hat\tau_{g,h} / \sum_g N^1_g$ (Section 3.3; with not-yet-treated controls it equals the Callaway–Sant'Anna estimator). Both identities are tests of the package.

  • A condition on a value that is not observed (a missing treatment, an absent period) is false: the row is left out.
  • With the default rule a treatment that ever switches off is an error, pointing to nonabsorbing. (lpdid runs, with a regressor of −1 on those rows.)
  • With a non-absorbing treatment (DGJT Section 4.2) the estimand is the effect of a treatment event under the assumption that effects stabilise within $L$ periods; with firsttreat and not-yet- or never-treated controls it is the effect of entering treatment for the first time.
  • The rules are evaluated on all the data, before subset: a row can use treatment values of rows that subset excludes, as with every lead and lag in panellp.

sample = :common keeps the same rows at every horizon (no composition change; lpdid's nocomp): the intersection of the samples of the requested horizons, pre-treatment ones included.

Reweighting and covariates

  • reweight = true without covariates and with only time effects is a weighted regression: each period's rows are weighted by $1/(1 - p_t)$, $p_t$ the share of treated rows of the period in the regression (times the observation weights, if any). Periods without a treated row or without a control row leave the sample.

  • With covariates or further fixed effects, direct inclusion (reweight = false) keeps the variance weights and assumes that effects do not vary with the covariates (Section 4.1.2). reweight = true is then regression adjustment (Section 4.1.1): the outcome model is fitted on the clean controls and

    \[\hat\beta_h = \frac{1}{N^1} \sum_{\text{treated}} \bigl(\Delta_h y_{it} - Z_{it}'\hat\gamma_h\bigr),\]

    with $Z$ the covariates and the fixed effects. The fit reports the treatment coefficient only, and stores its influence function as the score, so the variance of the estimator is Clustered(:unit; ssc = :none), with normal critical values (as lpdid). A treated row whose period (or other fixed-effect level, or value of a two-valued covariate) has no control row cannot be predicted and is left out. With several fixed-effect dimensions the prediction also needs the row's combination of levels to be connected to the controls', as any two-way prediction does.

  • The time effects may be group × time effects, fe(region) & fe(t) (comparisons within a region and period); reweight = true is then regression adjustment as well.

  • reweight = :lpdid is a compatibility switch for the weighted regression: see the audit page.

Base period, pooled estimates, averages

  • pmd = k (pre-mean differencing, Section 3.4) replaces the base $y_{i,t-1}$ by the mean of $y$ over $t-k, \dots, t-1$ (all required); pmd = :max by the mean over every earlier row of the unit. With a non-absorbing treatment the base window must also be free of switches, and :max means $k = L$. With pmd, $h = -1$ is estimated like any other horizon (lpdid reports it as zero).

  • pooled = true gives the pooled estimate (Section 3.5): one regression of the outcome averaged over the horizons, less the base, on the rows where the whole window is observed, with the clean-control rule of the far end of the window.

    post = panellp(f, data; id, time, horizon = 0:8, pooled = true, estimator = LPDiD())
pre = panellp(f, data; id, time, horizon = -5:-2, pooled = true, estimator = LPDiD())

  • averageeffect averages the event-study coefficients over the post-treatment horizons (and over $h \le -2$), weighting each by its number of treated rows: the average effect over all treated observations of the window, with the standard error of its influence function (which includes the estimated weights). Unlike the pooled estimate it does not need complete windows.

  • pretrendtest is the joint test that the pre-treatment coefficients are zero, from their covariance across horizons (the score sums by cluster are stored with global codes, so no regression is stacked).

Inference

The paper and lpdid cluster by unit: Clustered(:unit), with reghdfe's small-sample factor and t(G − 1) critical values, is lpdid's output. Any other specification applies to the weighted-regression and direct-inclusion fits. The wild bootstrap of lpdid is not implemented.
