Audit: lpdid

lpdid (Busch & Girardi; SSC S459273), version 1.1.0 of 20 September 2026, is the Stata implementation of LP-DiD by one of the paper's authors. It is GPL-3, so it was run as a black box and nothing of it is ported: LPDiD is written from Dube, Girardi, Jordà & Taylor (2025) and from the command's documented behaviour, and compared with its outputs on the synthetic panels fx_lpdid and fx_lpdid_unbal (crosscheck/stata/p7_lpdid.do, 47 option sets on each panel; test/test_crosscheck_p7.jl).

What it computes

Per horizon, reghdfe of the long difference on D.treat, the covariates and time effects, on the clean-control sample, clustered by unit; with rw and covariates, regression adjustment through listreg; optionally pooled regressions, an average of the event-study coefficients, a joint pre-trend test, and a wild bootstrap through boottest. The LP-DiD page maps every option to panellp.

Agreement

Event-study coefficients, standard errors, confidence bounds and numbers of observations agree to round-off (coefficients 1e-14, standard errors 5e-11) for every option set: covariates, lagged outcomes, extra fixed effects, weights, clusters, pmd, nevertreated, nocomp, the non-absorbing and one-off rules with notyet, firsttreat and untreated_before, rw by weighted regression and by regression adjustment; so do the pooled estimates, aggregate_average and pretrend_test.

Findings

  1. rw on unbalanced panels is not the equally weighted estimate. The weighted regression's factor $1/(1 - p_t)$ should use the treated share $p_t$ of the rows of the regression. lpdid 1.1.0 computes it on the rows that are clean and have the outcome, which includes rows whose $\Delta D$ is undefined (a unit's first row, a row after a gap) and which the regression then drops: the controls of those periods are overcounted. On fx_lpdid_unbal the coefficients differ from the equally weighted average of the cohorts' comparisons by up to 0.011 (0.2%); LPDiD(reweight = true) equals that average to round-off (a test), and equals a hand-written reghdfe with the weights from the regression rows (d_rw_hand in the do-file). On balanced panels, and with regression adjustment (which uses the regression rows), there is no difference. LPDiD(reweight = :lpdid) reproduces the command.
  2. An absorbing treatment is not checked. Without nonabsorbing(), a treatment that switches off stays in the sample with a regressor of −1. LPDiD() refuses it and points to nonabsorbing.
  3. nonabsorbing(0) is nonabsorbing(1). The same here (the rule needs $\Delta D_{t-1}$), stated in the table of clean controls.
  4. nocomp with a one-sided window still requires the pre-treatment horizon 2 (and, with only a pre window, horizon 0), outcomes included. sample = :common uses the requested horizons: pass horizon = -2:H for the command's sample.
  5. The pooled regressions under nocomp require the clean-control flags of both pooled windows. pooled = true with sample = :common uses the horizons of its own window.
  6. Mixed conventions in the output. The event-study standard errors carry reghdfe's small-sample factor and t(G − 1) critical values, the regression-adjustment ones no factor and normal critical values, and aggregate_average no factor with t(M − 1). averageeffect and pretrendtest follow the command; for the event study choose with Clustered(…; ssc = …).
  7. The pre-treatment row pre1 is always (0, missing), also under pmd(), where the difference between $y_{t-1}$ and the pre-treatment mean is not zero by construction. panellp estimates $h = -1$ like any other horizon then.
  8. The reported t and p are rounded (2 and 4 decimals); compare coefficients, standard errors and bounds.
  9. A pre-treatment difference that the lagged outcomes span (ylags(k): horizons up to $-k$; dylags(k): up to $-(k+1)$) is zero by construction: reghdfe returns round-off, regression adjustment (0, 0). Here the coefficient is round-off as well, and pretrendtest leaves those horizons out, as the command does.

Not compared: the wild bootstrap (bootstrap()), which is not implemented.
