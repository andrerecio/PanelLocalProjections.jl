Fixed-effect absorption

Fixed effects are removed by the within transformation on each horizon's own sample. The samples differ across horizons because leads run out at the end of each unit's series.

  • One dimension. Each column is demeaned by group once, which is exact.
  • Several dimensions (AlternatingProjections). Gauss–Seidel sweeps demean the dimensions in formula order. The limit is the residual of the projection on all the fixed-effect dummies (von Neumann–Halperin). After every two sweeps an Irons–Tuck step extrapolates along the last two updates. That direction lies in the span of the fixed effects, so the limit is unchanged. The step is skipped when the second difference is negligible relative to the first. Iteration stops when a sweep moves no element by more than tol · max|v₁| (default tol = 1e-12). Here v₁ is the column after its first sweep, which has already removed the column's level, so the rule depends on neither the units nor the level of the data. Rescaling a variable, or adding a constant to it, changes neither the estimates nor the number of sweeps. On slowly mixing panels, such as short rotating spells, the remaining error can exceed the last step by a factor $1/(1-\rho)$, where $\rho$ is the contraction rate of the sweeps. Non-convergence within maxiter sweeps is reported, with the horizons affected.
  • OneSweep. One pass per dimension. With fe(id) + fe(t) this is the unit-then-time demeaning of the Mei–Sheng–Shi replication code, which is exact only on balanced samples. It exists to reproduce their published numbers.

Absorbed degrees of freedom

$df_a$ is the rank of the fixed-effect dummies, counted as reghdfe does:

  • the levels of the first dimension;
  • for each later dimension, its levels minus $\max_{i<j} m_{ij}$. Here $m_{ij}$ is the number of connected components of the graph linking the groups of dimensions $i$ and $j$.

This is exact for one or two dimensions. With three or more it is an upper bound, and so gives conservative standard errors; reghdfe reports the same bound. The test suite checks $df_a$ against the rank of the dummy matrix (one and two dimensions, including a sample with two disconnected blocks) and against reghdfe's e(df_a) (one, two and three dimensions, nested clusters).

Absorbed and collinear regressors

A regressor is absorbed and omitted when its within-transformed squared norm is at most 1e-8 times its pre-absorption centred squared norm (a norm ratio of 1e-4): its coefficient is NaN and it is not counted in $K$. The test runs before any rescaling, which could otherwise inflate round-off into a spurious unit-norm column.

The remaining columns are scaled to unit norm and factorised by a pivoted QR. Columns beyond the numerical rank ($|R_{jj}| ≤ 10^{-9}|R_{11}|$) are omitted as collinear. Omitted regressors are reported once per call, with the horizons where they were omitted.

A shock that is absorbed or collinear in a horizon's sample makes that horizon :singular: its coefficients are NaN, and the horizons are reported. This happens, for example, with a staggered treatment dummy whose later horizons only see untreated periods. When the shock is not identified at any horizon panellp throws an error, as for an aggregate shock with time effects.

The design follows FixedEffectModels.jl (M. Gomez) and Regress.jl (G. Ragusa); the acceleration follows fixest (L. Bergé) and reghdfe (S. Correia).
