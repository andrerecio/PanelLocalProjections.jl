Audit: tidyMacro fLPPanel

tidyMacro (Muhsin Ciftci; MIT licence; version 1.1.0, commit dbf16c9, 19 October 2026) is an R package whose fLPPanel estimates the lag-augmented panel local projection of Almuzara & Sancibrián with clustered or Imbens–Kolesár inference. Its C++ engine describes its fixed-effect step as matching "regress_HDFE in the MATLAB / R source" (src/fLPPanel.cpp, l.35), the code of Almuzara & Sancibrián, which has no licence. This package neither uses nor runs tidyMacro. The findings below come from reading its source at that commit; line numbers refer to src/fLPPanel.cpp and R/fLPPanel.R.

What it computes

At each horizon, fLPPanel regresses $y_{i,t+h}$ (or $\sum_{r=0}^{h} y_{i,t+r}$ with cumulative = TRUE) on the interactions of the exposures with the aggregate shocks, $\min(h, p_{\max})$ lags of $y$ and of the interactions, and the controls, after absorbing the fixed effects. This is the authors' panel_LP, with the lag rule CappedLags(p_max) (no lag at $h = 0$, no information criterion). The variance is clustered by period, by unit or both, with ssc = "none", "CR1" or "IK" (the HC2 and Imbens–Kolesár refinement, by period only).

Findings

  • The degrees of freedom use the first coefficient's contrast for every coefficient (XX0_col0 = XtXt_pinv.col(0), l.370), as the authors' code does (see the audit of that code, bug B2). With several shocks, every degrees of freedom but the first is computed for the wrong contrast.
  • The synthetic weights are the raw exposures (l.336–347), as in the authors' code: $\bar X_t = \sum_{i \in t} s_i \ddot X_{it} / \sum_{i \in t} s_i^2$, with the periods unweighted. The paper uses the demeaned exposure, and TLAHR() weights the periods by $\omega_t$ (fn 24). The two differ on unbalanced panels, with time-varying exposures and with several exposures (see t-LAHR). The exposure column of coefficient is is is % n_s_cols (l.336), which fixes the out-of-range index of the authors' code with several aggregate shocks (bug B1).
  • The leverage comes from a pseudo-inverse of the Gram matrix $\bar X'\bar X$ (l.357–359), with Armadillo's default tolerance. When $\bar X$ is ill-conditioned this squares its condition number, and the dropped directions change the leverage and the degrees of freedom. The residual-maker diagonal enters as $\sqrt{|d_t|}$, floored at 1e-14 (l.362–364), so a leverage of one does not stop the computation.
  • Date time columns lose most periods (R/fLPPanel.R, l.47–61). Dates are converted to day counts and the grid step is the smallest gap between distinct dates. Rows more than 0.01 of a step off the grid are dropped. With quarterly dates the step is 90 days, so a quarter of 91 or 92 days sits 0.011–0.022 steps off; monthly dates (a 28-day step) fare worse. Integer periods are not affected.
  • The fixed-effect demeaning stops when the norm of a sweep's change is below 1e-8 times the Frobenius norm of $[y\ X]$ (l.108–120), after at most 10,000 sweeps, without acceleration. Non-convergence is reported in a status code.
  • Singletons are kept, as in the authors' code.
