Inference

Variances are computed after estimation, from score sums stored at every horizon. Many specifications can therefore be compared without re-estimating:

r = panellp(f, data; id = :id, time = :t, horizon = 10)
summarize(r, Clustered(:unit))                       # one-way, by unit
summarize(r, Clustered(:unit, :time))                # two-way (Cameron–Gelbach–Miller)
summarize(r, DriscollKraaySE())                      # Driscoll–Kraay
cv = vcov(Robust(), r); vcov(cv, 3)                  # the matrix at horizon 3

Robust(; ssc)heteroskedasticity-robust

Small-sample conventions

The reference implementations disagree on the small-sample factor and the critical values. Each convention is an explicit ssc value. Below, $n$ is the number of observations, $K$ the number of estimated coefficients, $df_a$ the absorbed degrees of freedom, $G$ the (smallest) number of clusters and $T$ the number of periods in the sample.

Clustered(:unit, :time), :mss$\frac{G}{G-1}\frac{n-1}{n-N-K}$, $G = \min(N, T)$normalMei–Sheng–Shi code, robust = T

The two :mss rows use $N$ and $T$, the numbers of units and periods in the data; ssc = :mss exists only for these two specifications, as in the authors' code. With unbalanced data the two-way factor turns negative at long horizons ($n \le N + K$), which is reported. ase in xtlp and xtscc removes the small-sample factor but keeps their $t(G-1)$ or $t(T-1)$ critical values. summarize(…; dist = :normal) gives normal ones.

A variance is undefined at a horizon with a single cluster or period, or without residual degrees of freedom. It is then NaN at that horizon, and the horizons are reported.

Fixed effects nested in a cluster dimension (for example unit effects with unit clusters) are not counted in $df_a^{c}$, as in reghdfe. If any is nested, $a = 1$ and the first remaining fixed effect is charged one level. $df_a^{dk}$ treats time as the cluster variable, without the $a$ term, as reghdfe vce(dkraay) and xtlp do. With three or more fixed effects, $df_a$ follows reghdfe's pairwise rule, which is a conservative upper bound.

Driscoll–Kraay bandwidth

bw sets the number of lags $m$ (weights $1 - j/(m+1)$):

bwlagssource
:nw94$\lfloor 4(T/100)^{2/9} \rfloor$xtscc, xtlp
:as2026$\lceil 4(T/100)^{2/9} \rceil - 1$Almuzara & Sancibrián's kernel bandwidth $M$ (weights $1 - \ell/M$)
:mss2026$\lfloor T^{1/4} \rfloor$Mei, Sheng & Shi, appendix B.2.2
integeras givenreghdfe vce(dkraay bw) uses bw = lags + 1

timelags = :calendar (the default) lags by calendar period, so a period absent from the sample contributes a zero sum. timelags = :position lags over the periods present, as xtlp, xtscc and reghdfe do.

Two-way variances that are not positive semi-definite

psd repairs a two-way variance by clipping negative eigenvalues to zero (Cameron, Gelbach & Miller 2011):

psdclippingas in
:stata (default for two dimensions)on $S V S$, with $S$ the diagonal of the raw regressors' standard deviations, floored at 1e-3. With an intercept, the slope block is clipped on its own and the intercept enters with scale 1reghdfe and xtlp
:rawon $V$ itselfthe Mei–Sheng–Shi code, eigens = T; fixest's default vcov_fix = TRUE (which floors eigenvalues at 1e-16)
:nonenonefixest with vcov_fix = FALSE

Without a repair, a two-way variance can have negative diagonal elements. Their standard errors are NaN, and the horizons are reported. To reproduce fixest's default two-way clustering, use psd = :raw.

Critical values

summarize uses each specification's critical values: $t$ with the degrees of freedom above, or normal. dist = :normal forces normal ones, e.g. to reproduce the ±1.96 bands of xtlp's e(irf) or of the Mei–Sheng–Shi figures.
