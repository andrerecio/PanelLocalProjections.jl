Panel LP-IV

When the macro shock of interest is not observed, but an endogenous aggregate $\tilde X_t$ (a policy rate) and a noisy proxy $X_t^* = a_0 X_t + \nu_t$ (high-frequency surprises) are, Almuzara & Sancibrián (2026, §3.4) instrument the former with the latter:

\[y_{i,t+h} = \beta^{IV}(h)\, s_i \tilde X_t + \psi(h)' W_{it} + \mu_i(h) + \nu_t(h) + \xi_{it}(h),\qquad s_i \tilde X_t \text{ instrumented by } s_i X_t^*,\]

with the normalisation $b_0 = 1$ (the impact response of $\tilde X_t$ to $X_t$). Under heterogeneous responses the estimand is the same as in the observed-shock case: the first stage is aggregate, so the instrument corrects measurement error without reweighting the units (their Proposition 3).

The fit

A block (endo ~ inst) in the formula makes every horizon's fit two-stage least squares on its fixed-effects design:

r = panellp(@formula(leads(y) ~ (s & ffr ~ s & mp) + w + fe(id) + fe(t)), data;
            id = :id, time = :t, horizon = 12)
  • The endogenous regressors are on the left of the block, the excluded instruments on its right; the other regressors are exogenous and instrument themselves. Several endogenous regressors are allowed (e.g. group dummies × an aggregate). They are listed first among the coefficients and are the default shock.
  • The instruments are absorbed with the rest of the design. The first stage projects the endogenous columns on the exogenous ones and the instruments, and the fitted values X̂ replace them; the residual is the structural one, $\tilde y - \tilde X \hat\beta$. The fit stores the bread $(\hat X'\hat X)^{-1}$ and the scores $\hat x_{it}\hat\xi_{it}$, so every variance (Robust, Clustered, DriscollKraaySE, CosineHAR, TLAHR) applies to the IV scores unchanged. In the just-identified case $(\hat X'\hat X)^{-1}\hat X' = (Z'X)^{-1}Z'$, which is their supplement A.4.
  • An instrument absorbed by the fixed effects (e.g. an aggregate series with fe(t)), or collinear with the exogenous regressors or an earlier instrument, is left out of the first stage, with a note. A horizon whose instruments do not identify every endogenous regressor (too few left, a first-stage partial R² of round-off size, or collinear fitted values) is :underidentified, with NaN coefficients.
  • IV works with WithinFE and LagAugmented. The split-panel jackknife and CD-OLS have no IV version.

t-LAHR for LP-IV

LagAugmented adds, at horizon $h$, $p(h)$ lags of the outcome (exogenous) and of the endogenous shock (endogenous, instrumented by the same lags of every instrument): their $W_{it} = (s_i\tilde X_{t-1}, y_{i,t-1}, \dots)$ and $W^*_{it} = (s_i X^*_{t-1}, y_{i,t-1}, \dots)$. Lag $\ell$ of the instruments enters only with lag $\ell$ of an endogenous shock, so the fit at $h$ depends only on $p(h)$.

TLAHR(refine = false) is their LP-IV variance: CR0 by period on the IV scores with normal critical values (supplement A.4). The HC2/Imbens–Kolesár refinement (supplement B.2) is defined for OLS only, so TLAHR(refine = true), the default, is an error on IV fits.

With ICLags, $\hat p$ is chosen on the reduced form: the regression of the outcome on the instruments in place of the endogenous regressors, as an OLS panel LP with the shock's instrument as its shock (the instrument with the endogenous shock's exposure, s & mp for s & ffr), whose exposure weights the criterion. This is an extension: the paper's criterion (supplement B.1) is stated for an observed shock.

First-stage diagnostics

firststage reports per horizon, with any variance specification (default TLAHR(refine = false)):

  • the Kleibergen–Paap (2006) rk Wald F, from the singular value decomposition of the normalised first stage, for one or several endogenous regressors;
  • the Montiel Olea–Pflueger (2013) effective F, for one endogenous regressor;
  • the first-stage coefficients of the instruments, net of the fixed effects and the exogenous regressors, with their standard errors.

The small-sample factor uses the first stage's degrees of freedom (exogenous regressors plus instruments), as ivreg2, ivreghdfe and weakivtest do; firststage reproduces ivreghdfe's widstat and weakivtest's F_eff (see Cross-checks).

The variance matters for the first stage as much as for the second. With an aggregate instrument the effective sample size is $T$: clustering by unit treats the $T$ values of the instrument as replicated across units and can report an F in the hundreds of thousands, where the F clustered by period reveals a weak instrument (the US state example).

fs = firststage(r)                          # CR0 by period
firststage(r; vcov = Clustered(:unit))      # for comparison
fs.kpF, fs.effF                             # per horizon
