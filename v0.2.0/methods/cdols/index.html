Cumulative-difference OLS

Ugarte-Ruiz (2026) avoids the within transformation altogether. Taking the long difference of the outcome removes the unit effect, so the local projection can be estimated by pooled OLS:

\[y_{i,t+h} - y_{i,t-1} = c_h + \beta_h s_{i,t} + \gamma_h s_{i,t-1} + \varepsilon_{i,t+h}, \qquad h = 0, \dots, H .\]

No unit effect is estimated, so the incidental-parameter (Nickell) bias of the fixed-effects estimator does not arise. Because $y_{i,t-1}$ is predetermined, $\beta_h$ is the response of the level $y_{i,t+h}$: the estimand of a fixed-effects LP in levels that also controls for $s_{i,t-1}$. A levels LP without the shock's lag (the paper's fixed-effects and split-panel jackknife comparators, its eq. 24) targets another quantity when both series are persistent: it converges to $\beta_h + \beta \rho_y^{h+1}\rho_s/(1 - \rho_y\rho_s)$, +178% of $\beta$ at $h = 0$ with $\rho_y = \rho_s = 0.8$ (a test item checks both limits on a long panel).

r = panellp(@formula(ldiff(y) ~ s + lags(s, 1)), data; id = :id, time = :t,
            horizon = 9, estimator = CDOLS())
summarize(r, Clustered(:unit))

The lag of the shock is required

With a persistent shock ($\rho_s \neq 0$), $s_{i,t}$ is correlated with the unobserved past of $y$ that remains in the long difference. Partialling out $s_{i,t-1}$ leaves the shock innovation, which is orthogonal to it. In the AR(1) design of the paper ($y_{it} = \rho_y y_{i,t-1} + \beta s_{it} + u_i + e_{it}$, $s_{it} = \rho_s s_{i,t-1} + v_{it}$):

quantityprobability limit
$\hat\beta_h$$\beta_h = \beta \sum_{j=0}^{h} \rho_y^{h-j} \rho_s^{j}$
$\hat\gamma_h$ (a nuisance coefficient)$(\rho_y^{h+1} - 1)\beta / (1 - \rho_y \rho_s)$
$\hat\beta_h$ without $s_{i,t-1}$$\beta_h - (1 - \rho_y^{h+1}) \beta \rho_s / (1 - \rho_y \rho_s)$

The test suite checks all three on large simulated panels. Omitting $s_{i,t-1}$ biases the response by −80% of $\beta$ at every horizon when only the shock is persistent ($\rho_s = 0.8$, $\rho_y = 0$), and by −44% at $h = 0$ to −198% at $h = 9$ when both are ($\rho_s = \rho_y = 0.8$; −47% to −309% at 0.9); not at all when $\rho_s = 0$. CDOLS() therefore requires it.

Specification rules

CDOLS() checks the formula (CDOLS(validate = false) skips the checks, e.g. for a pre-built outcome column):

  • the response is the long difference ldiff(y), or cumul(g) of a growth rate g ($\sum_{k=0}^{h} g_{i,t+k} = y_{i,t+h} - y_{i,t-1}$); cumul(firstdiff(y)) is ldiff(y);
  • every shock is dated $t$, right after the base period $t - 1$ (with a lagged response, ldiff(lags(y, ℓ)), it is dated $t - \ell$): a shock dated $t - 1$ against the base $y_{i,t-1}$ would estimate $\beta_{h+1} - \beta_0$;
  • the first lag of every shock is a regressor, however it is written (lags(s, 1), lags(s, 0:1) with shock = "L0.s", lags(firstdiff(s), 1), a pre-built column, …);
  • outcome-derived regressors are lagged beyond the response's start, and an outcome that the response differences enters only in differences, lags(firstdiff(y), 1:p) (lags of a growth rate g are allowed). A level $y_{i,t-1}$ contains the unit effect $u_i/(1 - \rho_y)$: it biases $\hat\beta_h$ when the unit effects are correlated with the shock, as in the paper's design;
  • no unit fixed effect, nor one nested in the units, in the data or in any horizon's sample (e.g. after subset or missing values): within estimation would bring the Nickell bias back. Time effects, fe(t), are allowed. A grouping with few units per group brings back part of that bias (about $1/g$ of it with $g$ units per group), so a warning reports groups that contain a single unit.

The paper also gives every control the same lags as the shock (a contemporaneous persistent control without its lag biases the response, like an omitted $s_{i,t-1}$); that is not checked. 0 + drops the intercept, as noconstant in locproj.

Inference

The paper reports "robust" standard errors without a formula. The long-difference error is serially correlated, so heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors are not valid. Simulating the paper's designs ($N = 100$, $T = 50$, signal-to-noise ratio 20; 2000 panels):

  • with $\rho_s = 0$, $\rho_y = 0.8$ (its Table 5), robust standard errors overstate the sampling standard deviation of $\hat\beta_h$ by 19%, 51% and 34% at $h = 2, 4, 9$, and robust 95% intervals cover 98–99.8% there: the paper's over-coverage;
  • with $\rho_s = \rho_y = 0.8$ (its main design), they understate it by 7–12% at $h = 1$–$4$ (coverage about 93%), and overstate it by 12% at $h = 9$.

Standard errors clustered by unit match the sampling standard deviation within 4% in both designs, with about 95% coverage. Clustering by unit is valid for a fixed $T$, Driscoll–Kraay for a large $T$. A test item checks these orderings with 200 panels.

When it fails

The probability limits above assume what the paper's design builds in:

  • a common mean and a common $\rho_s$ of the shock across units. With unit-specific shock means, the pooled regression of $s_{i,t}$ on $s_{i,t-1}$ leaves the mean in the residual, and CD-OLS is inconsistent. The test suite demonstrates a large bias;
  • $|\rho_y| < 1$ with a stationary start. With a unit root or unit-specific trends, the long difference keeps a unit term $(h+1)g_i$;
  • no feedback from $y$ to $s$.

The paper's Proposition 1 shows consistency, not finite-sample unbiasedness (see the audit).

Cross-checks

The tests compare CDOLS() with Stata (regress, xtscc, locproj …, m(reg) tr(cmlt) sl(1) [yl(1)]) and fixest, at horizons 0–9 on five panels:

  • the native design fx_cdols ($\rho_y = \rho_s = 0.8$, correlated effects);
  • three panels drawn by xtarsim (Bruno 2005), the generator of the paper's Monte Carlo, with $(\rho_s, \rho_y) \in \{(.8, 0), (0, .8), (.8, .8)\}$, $N = 100$, $T = 50$, signal-to-noise ratio 20 and the paper's correlated effects $u_i = 10(1 - \rho_y)(1 + \bar s_i - \bar s)$. The panels are committed, because the draws come from Stata's kiss32 generator. A test checks their moments against xtarsim's formulas, and another that the native design, at the same signal-to-noise ratio, reproduces them;
  • fx_unbalanced (shock x): gaps, singleton units, and a period absent for every unit (so no xtscc there, which refuses it).

Coefficients agree within 1e-12, standard errors within 5e-14, and observation counts, degrees of freedom and cluster counts exactly. The paper's comparators reproduce as well: xtreg, fe on the long difference (the paper's CMFE1), and xtlp …, m(spj) on the level response (the split-panel jackknife of the paper's applications; its Monte Carlo used locproj …, m(xtspj), which averages two splits when a unit has an odd number of rows and reports a within-OLS variance with normal critical values).
