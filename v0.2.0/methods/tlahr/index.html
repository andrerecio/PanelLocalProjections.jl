t-LAHR inference

Almuzara & Sancibrián (2026) study panel local projections of micro outcomes on a macro shock $X_t$ interacted with a unit-level exposure $s_i$:

\[y_{i,t+h} = \beta(h)\, s_i X_t + \psi(h)' W_{it} + \mu_i(h) + \nu_t(h) + \xi_{it}(h),\]

with unit and time effects (time effects only when $s_i$ varies; with $s_i \equiv 1$ they would absorb $X_t$). The estimand is the slope of the best linear predictor of the unit responses on the exposure, $\beta(h) = \operatorname{Cov}(s_i, \beta_{ih}) / \operatorname{Var}(s_i)$. Aggregate shocks make the errors dependent across units, so the effective sample size is $T$, not $NT$. Their recommendation, t-LAHR, is

  • time-clustered: the variance is clustered by period (CR0, eq. 11);
  • lag-augmented: the regression includes $p(h)$ lags of the outcome and of the shock, $W_{it} = (s_i X_{t-1}, y_{i,t-1}, \dots, s_i X_{t-p}, y_{i,t-p}, \tilde W_{it})$. With $p \ge h$ the scores $X_t \xi_t$ are serially uncorrelated (their Lemma A.1), so no kernel or bandwidth is needed;
  • heteroskedasticity-robust: the variance is the Eicker–Huber–White variance of the synthetic time-series regression (Remark 1), refined in small samples with HC2 and Student-t critical values with the Imbens–Kolesár degrees of freedom (supplement B.2).

It is not a HAR estimator: Driscoll–Kraay, equal-weighted cosine (EWC) and clustering by unit or two-way are the paper's comparators.

r = panellp(@formula(leads(y) ~ s & X + fe(id) + fe(t)), data; id = :id, time = :t,
            horizon = 12, estimator = LagAugmented())       # ICLags(): eq. (15)
summarize(r, TLAHR(); level = 0.90)                         # HC2 + Imbens–Kolesár t(ν)
lagorder(r), r.lags                                         # p(h); the criterion
synthetic(r, 4)                                             # the synthetic series at h = 4

Lag augmentation

LagAugmented adds, at each horizon, $p(h)$ lags of the outcome and of every shock column (named L1.y, L1.(s & X), …). A lag already among the regressors is not added again, whatever its name: columns are compared numerically, so with the shocks s & lags(X, 0:2) the lag of s & L0.X is s & L1.X. A lag that the formula's columns span is left out of the regression, without a warning: with lags(y, 1:2) in the formula, the lag $\Delta y_{t-1} = y_{t-1} - y_{t-2}$ that ldiff(y) adds is redundant, and the formula's two coefficients stay. A lag of a shock lags the whole column, so a time-varying exposure enters as $s_{i,t-\ell} X_{t-\ell}$, as in the authors' code (the paper writes $s_i X_{t-\ell}$). The information criterion

The paper chooses $\hat p$ on the synthetic time series (fn 11; supplement B.1): a panel criterion with penalty in $(NT)^{-1}$ over-fits, because redundant lags improve the fit at the rate $T^{-1}$. The paper leaves several details open; ICLags() pins them:

  • the "panel VAR(p)" is the $h = 0$ regression of the outcome on the formula's right-hand side (with the contemporaneous shock) and $p$ of its own lags, with the formula's fixed effects: B.3 adds one parameter per lag, the lag of $\bar Y$ (vars = :outcome_and_shock also adds the shock's lags, with penalty $p(1 + n_{\text{shock}})g$);
  • each candidate $p$ is fitted on its own sample, the rows where its lags are defined (B.4 sums over $t = p+1, \dots, T$; sample = :common uses the $p_{\max}$-lag sample for all);
  • the residuals are averaged per period with the synthetic weights of the first shock (below), $\bar\xi_t(p)$, and $\hat\sigma^2(p) = \sum_t \omega_t \bar\xi_t(p)^2 / \sum_t \omega_t$ over the periods with positive weight $\omega_t$: the mean square residual of the synthetic regression, which fn 24 weights by $\omega_t$. On a balanced panel with a time-invariant exposure $\omega_t$ is constant and $\hat\sigma^2$ is the mean of $\bar\xi_t^2$, as in B.4. Unweighted, the periods with few units, whose $\bar\xi_t$ have variance $\propto 1/\omega_t$, would dominate the criterion (with staggered entry it then often selects no lag, or $p_{\max}$, for a strong AR(2));
  • $\mathrm{IC}(p) = \log\hat\sigma^2(p) + k(p)\,g$, with $k(p)$ the lags candidate $p$ adds to the regression, $g = 2/T$ (AIC) or $\log T / T$ (BIC) and $T$ the periods of the $p = 0$ regression. A lag already in the formula (or spanned by it) adds nothing: candidates that differ only by such lags are the same regression, and $\hat p$ is the last of them, so writing lags(y, 1) in the formula changes neither $\hat p$ nor $p(h)$. Other ties go to the smaller $p$;
  • $p_{\max}$ is not given in the paper. The default is $\max\{1, \lceil (T - H)^{1/3} \rceil\}$, the authors' code's $p_{\max}$ ($T$ the distinct periods in the data, $H$ the largest horizon): with $T = 124$ and $H = 20$ it is 5; the paper's applications select up to 4.

The criterion needs the first shock to be an aggregate series times an exposure; for other shocks use ConstantLags or CappedLags.

Sensitivity

On the cross-checks' panels (fx_tlahr, fx_tlahr_unbal: a first-order design) every option of the criterion (criterion = :bic, sample = :common, vars = :outcome_and_shock, pmax 2 or 6) selects $\hat p = 1$, as ICLags(); on the test suite's AR(2) panel (ar2panel() in test/test_tlahr.jl: $N = 200$, $T = 60$, autoregressive coefficients 1.2 and −0.5) every option selects $\hat p = 2$. The variance

CR0 by period

TLAHR(refine = false) is the variance clustered by period with no small-sample factor and normal critical values, eq. (11):

\[\hat\sigma^2(h) = \frac{\sum_t \big(\sum_i \tilde x_{it}\hat\xi_{it}\big)^2} {\big(\sum_{i,t} \tilde x_{it}^2\big)^2},\]

$\tilde x_{it}$ being the shock residualised on the other regressors and the fixed effects. It equals Clustered(:time; ssc = :none). With WithinFE and no lags it is the paper's "t-HR" comparator.

HC2 and Imbens–Kolesár degrees of freedom

TLAHR() transfers the small-sample refinements of heteroskedasticity-robust inference to the synthetic regression (supplement B.2). For a shock with weights $w_i$:

  1. the synthetic regressors are the per-period weighted averages of the within-transformed regressors, $\bar X_t = \sum_{i \in t} w_i \ddot X_{it} / \omega_t$, $\omega_t = \sum_{i \in t} w_i^2$, one row per period of the sample;
  2. the synthetic regression is weighted by $\omega_t$ (fn 24), so its rows are $\tilde X_t = \sqrt{\omega_t}\,\bar X_t$, and $d_t = 1 - h_t$, with $h_t$ the leverage of period $t$ in $\tilde X$;
  3. the variance is $B\,(\sum_t S_t S_t' / d_t)\,B$, with $S_t$ the scores summed in period $t$ and $B$ the bread of the panel regression;
  4. the critical value is $t_{1-\alpha/2}(\nu_k)$, with the Bell–McCaffrey degrees of freedom of coefficient $k$ under a homoskedastic reference (Imbens & Kolesár 2016): $\nu_k = (\sum\lambda)^2 / \sum\lambda^2$, $\lambda$ the eigenvalues of $G'G$, $G_{\cdot t} = \bar M_{\cdot t} a_t / \sqrt{d_t}$, $a = \tilde X (\tilde X'\tilde X)^{-1} e_k$, $\bar M = I - \tilde X (\tilde X'\tilde X)^{-1} \tilde X'$. Since $G'G = D \bar M D$ with $D = \operatorname{diag}(a / \sqrt d)$, $\sum\lambda = \sum_t a_t^2$ and $\sum\lambda^2 = \sum_{t,s} a_t^2 a_s^2 \bar M_{ts}^2 / (d_t d_s)$, computed without an eigendecomposition.

The period weights. B.2 normalises the averages by $\sum_i \hat s_i^2$, which is constant over time on a balanced panel, and does not say what to do when $\omega_t$ varies (unbalanced panels, time-varying exposures). When every regressor is an aggregate series times the exposure, $\ddot X_{it} = w_i \bar X_t'$, the block of period $t$ in the panel's hat matrix has rank one, and its eigenvalue is the leverage of $\sqrt{\omega_t}\,\bar X_t$: the HC2 variance of the weighted synthetic regression is then CR2 clustered by period (Bell & McCaffrey 2002), and $\nu_k$ its Bell–McCaffrey degrees of freedom. A test checks both on an unbalanced panel, to 1e-12. The authors' code uses the unweighted $\bar X_t$, which gives a period with few units the leverage of a full one; TLAHR(weights = :raw) reproduces it. On a balanced panel with a time-invariant exposure $\omega_t$ is constant and the two coincide.

Stata. $\nu_k$ is, by construction, the degrees of freedom of Stata's regress …, vce(hc2, dfadjust) (e(adj_df)) on the weighted synthetic regression. The standard error is the panel's, $B\,(\sum_t S_t S_t'/d_t)\,B$; it equals that regression's HC2 standard error only when the panel regression is itself a synthetic regression (every regressor an aggregate series times the exposure, on a balanced panel), as in the cross-checks below. With the lagged outcome that LagAugmented adds, a unit-level regressor, they differ by a few percent (the paper's Remark 1 says "≈").

Numerics. $\tilde X'\tilde X$ is never formed: $\tilde X$ has a thin SVD, and singular values below $\max(T, K)\,\varepsilon\,\sigma_1$ are dropped (a comment in the authors' code notes that $\bar X'\bar X$ can be near-singular, $\kappa \approx 10^{11}$, and that a looser pseudo-inverse then makes the degrees of freedom depend on the truncation rank). With $U$ its left singular vectors and $b = a^2/d$, $\sum\lambda^2 = \sum_t b_t^2 d_t^2 + \lVert U' \operatorname{diag}(b)\, U \rVert_F^2 - \sum_t b_t^2 h_t^2$: no $T \times T$ matrix is formed, so the memory is linear in the number of periods (daily panels of thousands of periods). A period with leverage one (as many periods as synthetic regressors, without unit effects) makes the variance undefined: NaN, with a warning.

The absorbed intercept. B.2 builds $\bar X$ from the two-way within-transformed regressors, without a constant. On a balanced panel with time-invariant weights and unit effects, the columns of $\bar X$ sum to zero over the periods, so the leverages add up to at most $\operatorname{rank}(\bar X) \le T - 1$ and every $d_t \ge 1/T$: the synthetic regression behaves as one with an intercept whose leverage is not counted.

The weights

The paper's weights are the demeaned exposure, $\hat s_i = s_i - \bar s$, period by period when $s_{it}$ varies (Remark 3). TLAHR() uses, for a regressor $s_k X_t$, its exposure residualised per period

  • on an intercept, when a fixed effect nested in the periods is absorbed (time effects, or group × time effects, within whose groups it is demeaned), and
  • on the exposures of the other regressors that multiply the same aggregate series (up to scale), shocks or not.

The second rule extends the paper, which covers a scalar exposure. With several exposures of one shock (e.g. turnover in two liquidity groups, as in Jeenas & Lagos 2024), the raw exposures make $\bar X$ nearly singular. By Frisch–Waugh–Lovell, the coefficient on $s_k X_t$ is identified by $s_k$ net of the other exposures of $X_t$. The panel's residual is global, with a coefficient weighted by $X_t^2$; the residual period by period equals it on a balanced panel with time-invariant exposures, where the other exposures' synthetic columns then vanish (up to rounding, which the rank tolerance drops). Since the rule follows the regressors, not their labels, the inference on $s_k X_t$ does not depend on which of them shock names. With time-varying exposures or an unbalanced panel the other columns do not vanish: the contemporaneous ones survive through the unit effects, and the lags of the other exposure that LagAugmented adds stay nearly collinear with the shock's own lags. $\bar X$ is then ill-conditioned and $\nu$ low: with two lags and a time-varying second exposure on the cross-checks' balanced panel, $\nu$ of the first exposure is about 4.9 from $h = 3$ on, against 6.2–6.8 with the raw exposures and 8.1–9.6 with that exposure alone (at $h = 0$, without lags: 11.8, 4.5 and 12.7). These intervals err on the wide side. A period whose residualised exposure vanishes (no more units than exposures, or one exposure common to all units under time effects) has weight exactly zero, not a round-off weight.

TLAHR(weights = :raw) weights by the raw exposure $s_{it}$ and leaves the periods unweighted, as the authors' code. The two agree on balanced panels with time effects and a time-invariant exposure (where the within-transformed regressors sum to zero in each period, the weights differ by a constant and $\omega_t$ is constant), and for $s \equiv 1$ on balanced panels. They differ on unbalanced panels, with time-varying exposures, and with several exposures. On the cross-checks' unbalanced panel, the shock's standard errors differ by up to 3% and its degrees of freedom by up to 16%; with a time-varying exposure, by up to 4% and 29% (1% and 5% on the balanced panel).

Several shocks and the other coefficients

Every coefficient takes its $d_t$ and degrees of freedom from one synthetic regression:

  • a shock, its own, as in the authors' code (which reports one variance per shock);
  • a lag of a shock (L1.(s & X), however it was added), and a regressor with the same exposure as a shock (s & L1.X for the shock s & X), the shock's;
  • any other aggregate series times an exposure (e.g. a control s2 & X), its own;
  • every other coefficient (outcome lags, unit-level controls), the first shock's.

Writing $z^{(j)}_t = (B S_t)_j / \sqrt{d^{(c(j))}_t}$ for coefficient $j$ with owner $c(j)$, the variance is $V_{jl} = \sum_t z^{(j)}_t z^{(l)}_t$. Each coefficient's own variance is the paper's HC2 variance, and $V$ is positive semi-definite. The degrees of freedom of coefficient $j$ use its own contrast $e_j$; the authors' code uses $e_1$ for every coefficient (see the audit).

TLAHR() needs every shock to be an aggregate series (equal across units in every period), or an interaction of aggregate series with unit-level variables: s & X, X & s, log(X) & s & w, lags(s & X, 0:1), X alone (exposure 1). For any other shock, TLAHR() is an error and TLAHR(refine = false) gives the CR0 variance. Neither is defined for SplitPanelJackknife results.

Unit-level controls

B.2 averages the whole regressor vector, so each regressor enters the synthetic regression and takes up leverage there, unit-level controls included, although the panel identifies their coefficients from cross-sectional variation. Irrelevant controls therefore widen the refined intervals. On fx_tlahr with one lag, twenty columns of noise raise the shock's refined standard error by about two thirds, at $h = 0$ (0.096 to 0.158) and at $h = 4$ (0.256 to 0.421), and lower $\nu$ at $h = 4$ from 10.4 to 7.5, while the coefficient and the CR0 standard error do not move. Include the controls the specification needs, no more; TLAHR(refine = false) does not depend on them.

The synthetic series

synthetic(r, h) returns, per period of the horizon's sample, the weighted averages of the within-transformed shock (x), response (y) and residuals (resid), and the weight $\omega_t = \sum_i w_i^2$; synthetic(r, h; term) gives the column of any coefficient in the synthetic regression it uses (above). The local projection is approximately the time-series regression of y on x weighted by $\omega_t$ (fn 24); the paper plots the rescaled series $\sqrt{\omega_t}\,\hat Y_{t+h}$ and $\sqrt{\omega_t}\,X_t$ over time (Fig. 3).

Cross-checks

  • The authors' panel_LP.R, run on our two synthetic panels (balanced, and with 10% of the rows removed): five specifications ($s_i$ with unit and time effects; $s \equiv 1$ with unit effects; a time-varying exposure; a control; two exposures), $p_{\max} \in \{0, 2\}$, level and cumulative responses, with and without the refinement: 672 rows. With LagAugmented(CappedLags(pmax)), singletons = :keep and TLAHR(weights = :raw), they agree on the balanced panel within 2e-12 (estimates; standard errors 1e-13, degrees of freedom 3e-13, relative) and on the unbalanced one within 2e-9 (their absorption stops at a tolerance of 1e-8). The exception is the second exposure's degrees of freedom, where their code uses the first coefficient's contrast. The paper's weights agree with theirs where $\omega_t$ is constant (above).
  • Stata. reghdfe reproduces the lag-augmented two-way regression and its time-clustered variance (CR1, with the observation, cluster and degrees-of-freedom counts), within 2e-14 (the unbalanced panel's standard errors within 5e-12).
