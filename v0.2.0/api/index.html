API

Estimation

PanelLocalProjections.panellp — Function
panellp(f, data; id, time, horizon, estimator = WithinFE(), shock = nothing,
        frequency = nothing, subset = nothing, sample = :horizon, singletons = :drop,
        absorb = AlternatingProjections(), clusters = ())

Estimate a panel local projection: for each horizon h, regress the response defined by the left-hand side of f on its right-hand side, absorbing the fe() terms.

Formula

  • Left-hand side: leads(y) (y{i,t+h}), ldiff(y) (y{i,t+h} − y{i,t−1}), anchor(y, z) (y{i,t+h} − z{i,t}), cumul(y) (Σ{k≤h} y_{i,t+k}), or a plain column (a pre-built outcome) with horizon = 0.
  • Right-hand side: columns and functions of columns, interactions s & X, lags(x, p), lags(x, a:b), firstdiff(x), and fixed effects fe(v), fe(a) & fe(b). An intercept is included when there are no fixed effects (unless 0 +).
  • Panel LP-IV: one block (endo ~ inst), e.g. (s & ffr ~ s & mp), makes the fit two-stage least squares (Almuzara & Sancibrián 2026, §3.4) on each horizon's fixed-effects design: the endogenous regressors on the left (listed first among the coefficients, and the default shock), the excluded instruments on the right; the other regressors are exogenous and instrument themselves. Every variance applies to the IV scores X̂ ξ̂ (ξ̂ the structural residual); TLAHR(refine = false) is the paper's LP-IV variance. An instrument absorbed by the fixed effects or collinear with the exogenous regressors (or with earlier instruments) is left out of the first stage, with a note; a horizon with fewer instruments than endogenous regressors left is :underidentified. With WithinFE or LagAugmented (whose lags of an endogenous shock are instrumented by lags of the instruments).

Keywords

  • id, time: the unit and period columns. Integer periods are consecutive integers; Date/DateTime periods need frequency (:year, :halfyear, :quarter, :month). fe(time) and clusters on time group by period, not by date.
  • horizon: H (horizons 0:H) or a range of consecutive horizons such as 1:6.
  • estimator: WithinFE (fixed effects; pooled OLS without them), SplitPanelJackknife, CDOLS or LagAugmented.
  • shock: coefficient name(s) of the shock; by default the columns of the first regressor (the endogenous ones with an IV block). Required when the formula has interaction terms and other regressors.
  • subset: a Bool column or vector restricting the estimation sample after leads and lags are computed (they may read outside it).
  • sample: :horizon (each horizon its own complete cases) or :common (the intersection over horizons).
  • singletons: :drop (iteratively, as reghdfe and xtlp) or :keep.
  • absorb: AlternatingProjections or OneSweep.
  • clusters: extra columns (or tuples of columns, for intersections) whose score sums are stored for clustered variances, e.g. clusters = (:region, (:sector, :quarter)); unit, time and the fixed-effect dimensions always are.

Leads, lags and differences are taken on the (unit, calendar period) grid: an absent period makes them undefined, and rows where the response or any regressor is undefined (missing, NaN, or a missing input of a function term) leave that horizon's sample.

Horizons without observations, horizons where the shock is absorbed by the fixed effects or collinear in the horizon's sample (status :singular), omitted regressors and non-convergence are reported in one warning each per call, and their coefficients are NaN; it is an error when no horizon identifies the shock.

PanelLocalProjections.SplitPanelJackknife — Type
SplitPanelJackknife(; split = :ceil)

Split-panel jackknife correction of the fixed-effects estimator (Mei, Sheng & Shi 2026, eq. 16): θ̂ = 2θ̂full − ½(θ̂a + θ̂_b). The halves are formed per unit on each horizon's estimation sample, from its rows in time order:

  • split = :ceil puts the first ⌈n_i/2⌉ rows in half a, as the authors' R code and xtlp do;
  • split = :floor puts the first ⌊ni/2⌋ there, the paper's 𝒯a = {t ≤ T_h/2}.

Every fixed effect, time effects included, is re-estimated inside each half. The variance uses the full-sample bread, the SPJ residuals and the scores d = 2X̃ − X̃half, which reduce to the paper's d* on balanced panels with an even number of periods. Requires a unit fixed effect, fe(id). A horizon where a half is rank-deficient (a regressor absorbed inside it) is `:spjhalf_singular`, with NaN coefficients.

PanelLocalProjections.CDOLS — Type
CDOLS(; validate = true)

Cumulative-difference OLS (Ugarte-Ruiz 2026): pooled OLS, with an intercept (unless 0 +) and without unit effects, of the long difference y{i,t+h} − y{i,t−1} on the shock and its first lag. The long difference removes the unit effect, so there is no within transformation and no Nickell bias.

With validate = true the formula must follow the method:

  • the response is ldiff(y), or cumul(g) of a growth rate g;
  • each shock is dated t, right after the base period t − 1 of the long difference (t − ℓ with a lagged response, ldiff(lags(y, ℓ))), and its first lag is a regressor, however it is written (lags(s, 1), lags(s, 0:1), …);
  • the outcome enters the right-hand side only lagged beyond the response's start and, when the response differences it (ldiff(y), cumul(firstdiff(y))), only in differences, lags(firstdiff(y), 1:p): a level of y carries the unit effect;
  • no fixed effect is nested in the units, in the data or in any horizon's sample (time effects are allowed); fixed-effect groups with a single unit give a warning.

The paper also gives every control the lags of the shock (it is not checked). validate = false skips the checks, e.g. for a pre-built long-difference column.

PanelLocalProjections.LagAugmented — Type
LagAugmented(rule = ICLags())

Lag-augmented fixed-effects estimator of Almuzara & Sancibrián (2026): at horizon h, the regression adds p(h) lags of the outcome and of every shock column to the formula (rule sets p(h)), so that the scores are serially uncorrelated and inference clustered by period is valid (TLAHR). The lagged outcome is y for leads(y) and anchor(y, z), Δy for ldiff(y) and leads(firstdiff(y)), and g for cumul(g). Lags of a shock lag the whole column (s{i,t−ℓ}X{t−ℓ} for an exposure that varies over time). A lag already among the regressors, whatever its name (columns are compared numerically: s & L1.X is the lag of the shock s & L0.X), is not added again; one that the formula's columns span (Δy lags with lags(y, 1:2) in the formula) is left out of the regression. Requires unit fixed effects, fe(id), or finer ones nested in the units (e.g. fe(id) & fe(state)).

With an IV block (panel LP-IV, Almuzara & Sancibrián 2026, §3.4), the lags of an endogenous shock are endogenous, instrumented by the same lags of every excluded instrument; the lags of the outcome are exogenous. ICLags then works on the reduced form, the instruments in place of the endogenous regressors.

PanelLocalProjections.ICLags — Type
ICLags(; criterion = :aic, pmax = nothing, vars = :outcome, sample = :perp)

Almuzara–Sancibrián (2026) rule p(h) = max{1, min(h, p̂)} (eq. 15; supplement B.7), with p̂ chosen by an information criterion on the synthetic time series (fn 11; supplement B.3–B.4). For each p = 0, …, pmax, the outcome is regressed at h = 0 on the formula's right-hand side and p of its own lags, with the formula's fixed effects; the residuals are averaged across units with the first shock's exposure weights, ξ̄_t(p), and

IC(p) = log σ̂²(p) + k(p) · g,   σ̂²(p) = Σ_t ω_t ξ̄_t(p)² / Σ_t ω_t,

with ωt the synthetic weight of period t (constant on balanced panels with a time-invariant exposure, where σ̂² is the mean of ξ̄t²), k(p) the lags candidate p adds to the regression (none for a lag already among the regressors, or spanned by them), and g = 2/T (:aic) or log(T)/T (:bic), T the periods of the p = 0 sample. Ties go to the smaller p, except between candidates that are the same regression (their lags are already in the formula): p̂ is then the larger, so the formula's lags count towards it.

  • pmax: largest candidate; default max{1, ⌈(T − H)^{1/3}⌉}, T the distinct periods in the data and H the largest horizon.
  • vars = :outcome_and_shock also adds p lags of each shock to candidate p (each counted in k(p)).
  • sample = :common fits every candidate on the pmax-lag sample.

For panel LP-IV the criterion is that of the reduced form: the excluded instruments take the place of the endogenous regressors, as if the formula were the OLS regression of the outcome on them, and the instrument with the exposure of the (first) endogenous shock (s & mp for s & ffr) is its shock, whose exposure weights the residuals. With no such instrument, use ConstantLags or CappedLags.

PanelLocalProjections.CappedLags — Type
CappedLags(pmax = nothing)

p(h) = min(h, pmax): no lag at h = 0. The rule of the Almuzara–Sancibrián code (panel_LP.m/panel_LP.R), whose default pmax is ⌈(T − H)^{1/3}⌉ (T the distinct periods in the data, H the largest horizon). A compatibility switch: the paper recommends ICLags.

PanelLocalProjections.AlternatingProjections — Type
AlternatingProjections(; tol = 1e-12, maxiter = 10_000, accelerate = true)

Within transformation by alternating projections (Gauss–Seidel sweeps over the fixed-effect dimensions), with an Irons–Tuck extrapolation after every two sweeps when accelerate. A single dimension is demeaned exactly in one pass. Iteration stops when a sweep moves no element by more than tol · max|v₁|, where v₁ is the column after its first sweep (which has removed its level): the rule does not depend on the units or the level of the data. On slowly mixing panels the remaining error can exceed the last step by a factor 1/(1 − ρ), with ρ the convergence rate of the sweeps.

source
PanelLocalProjections.OneSweep — Type
OneSweep()

One demeaning pass per fixed-effect dimension, in formula order. With fe(id) + fe(t) this is the unit-then-time sweep of the Mei–Sheng–Shi replication code (LP_panel_all.r), which is the exact two-way within transformation only on balanced samples. A compatibility switch for reproducing published numbers; prefer AlternatingProjections.

Inference

PanelLocalProjections.Robust — Type
Robust(; ssc = :stata)

Heteroskedasticity-robust (Eicker–Huber–White) variance. ssc = :none is HC0; :stata is HC1 with the factor n/(n − K − df_a) of regress, vce(robust), reghdfe and xtlp vce(robust), and t(n − K − df_a) critical values.

source
PanelLocalProjections.Clustered — Type
Clustered(dims...; ssc = :stata, psd = length(dims) == 2)

Cluster-robust variance, one- or two-way (Cameron–Gelbach–Miller). Each dimension is :unit, :time, a fixed-effect or declared cluster column, or a tuple of columns for their intersection (e.g. (:sic3, :quarter)).

ssc sets the small-sample correction:

  • :none: no factor; normal critical values.
  • :stata: (n−1)/(n − a − K − df_a_eff) · G/(G−1) with G the smallest number of clusters, absorbed effects nested in a cluster dimension not counted (a = 1 if any is); t(G − 1) critical values. As xtlp, reghdfe, xtreg, fe vce(cluster).
  • :mss: N/(N−1) · (n−1)/(n−K) for Clustered(:unit), or G/(G−1) · (n−1)/(n−N−K) with G = min(N, T) for Clustered(:unit, :time), with N and T the numbers of units and periods in the data, as in the Mei–Sheng–Shi replication code with robust = T; normal critical values. Only these two specifications exist in that code.

psd repairs a two-way variance that is not positive semi-definite by clipping its negative eigenvalues to zero:

  • :stata (or true, the default for two dimensions) clips in the metric scaled by the standard deviations of the raw regressors (floored at 1e-3), as reghdfe and xtlp do. With an intercept, the slopes are clipped on their own and the intercept enters the metric with scale 1, as in reghdfe.
  • :raw clips V itself, as the Mei–Sheng–Shi code with eigens = T and fixest's default vcov_fix = TRUE (which floors the eigenvalues at 1e-16 instead of 0).
  • :none (or false) leaves V unchanged; a negative variance then has a NaN standard error.
source
PanelLocalProjections.DriscollKraaySE — Type
DriscollKraaySE(bw = :nw94; ssc = :stata, timelags = :calendar)

Driscoll–Kraay variance: Bartlett-weighted long-run variance of the cross-sectional sums of the scores, ordered by calendar period.

bw sets the number of lags m (weights 1 − j/(m+1)):

  • :nw94: ⌊4(T/100)^{2/9}⌋ (xtscc, xtlp).
  • :as2026: ⌈4(T/100)^{2/9}⌉ − 1, Almuzara–Sancibrián's kernel bandwidth M = ⌈⋯⌉ with weights 1 − j/M.
  • :mss2026: ⌊T^{1/4}⌋ (Mei–Sheng–Shi, appendix B.2.2).
  • an integer.

ssc:

  • :none: no factor; normal critical values.
  • :stata: (n−1)/(n − K − df_a^{DK}) · T/(T−1), as xtlp's default; t(T − 1).
  • :xtscc: (n−1)/(n − K − 1{FE}) · T/(T−1), as xtscc and xtlp …, nodfadj; t(T − 1).

timelags = :position lags over the periods present in the sample (as xtlp/xtscc) instead of calendar periods.

source
PanelLocalProjections.TLAHR — Type
TLAHR(; refine = true, weights = :residual)

t-LAHR inference of Almuzara & Sancibrián (2026): the variance clustered by period, with no small-sample factor, of a lag-augmented regression (LagAugmented), whose scores are serially uncorrelated.

  • refine = false: CR0 by period (eq. 11), normal critical values. Equal to Clustered(:time; ssc = :none); with WithinFE and no lags, the paper's "t-HR".
  • refine = true (the paper's recommendation, supplement B.2): HC2 with the leverage of the synthetic time-series regression, and Student-t critical values with the Bell–McCaffrey/Imbens–Kolesár degrees of freedom ν of each coefficient. Needs every shock to be an aggregate series times a unit-level exposure (s & X, or X alone).

weights sets the synthetic regressions. :residual (the paper's) averages with the exposure residualised per period, on an intercept when a time-type fixed effect is absorbed (the paper's ŝi) and on the exposures of the other regressors of the same aggregate series, and weights each period by ωt = Σi wi² (fn 24: with every regressor an aggregate series times the exposure, the HC2 variance is then CR2 clustered by period). :raw averages with the raw exposure and leaves the periods unweighted, as the authors' code (panel_LP.m/.R). They agree on balanced panels with time effects and a time-invariant exposure. A shock and its lags use the shock's synthetic regression, another aggregate series times an exposure its own, every other coefficient the first shock's.

source
PanelLocalProjections.CosineHAR — Type
CosineHAR(B = :llsw)

Equal-weighted cosine (EWC) variance of the scores summed by period (Lazarus, Lewis, Stock & Watson 2018): Ω = B⁻¹ Σ{j=1}^{B} Λj Λj', Λj = √2 Σt cos(πj(t − ½)/T) St, over the calendar periods from the first to the last of the sample (absent ones contribute zero); Student-t(B) critical values; no small-sample factor. B = :llsw is ⌊0.41 T^{2/3}⌋, as Stata's harreg; an integer fixes it. A comparator of TLAHR (the paper's "t-HAR").

source
StatsAPI.vcov — Method
vcov(spec::PanelVcov, r::PanelLocalProjection) -> PanelLPVcov

Variance of the coefficients at every horizon, for a Robust, Clustered, DriscollKraaySE, TLAHR or CosineHAR specification. Omitted coefficients have NaN rows and columns. Horizons where the variance is undefined (a single cluster or period, no residual degrees of freedom, or a period with leverage one in the t-LAHR synthetic regression) are NaN, with a warning; so are, in the standard errors, negative variances of a two-way variance without PSD repair.

PanelLocalProjections.firststage — Function
firststage(r; vcov = TLAHR(refine = false)) -> FirstStageSummary

First-stage diagnostics of a panel LP-IV result, per horizon, with the variance specification vcov (any of Robust, Clustered, DriscollKraaySE, CosineHAR, TLAHR(refine = false)) applied to the first-stage scores, with its small-sample factor computed from the first stage's degrees of freedom (its regressors: the exogenous ones and the excluded instruments), as ivreg2, ivreghdfe and weakivtest do:

  • kpF: the Kleibergen–Paap (2006) rk Wald F for weak identification, rk/L, with rk the Wald statistic of the test that Π (instruments × endogenous, net of the fixed effects and the exogenous regressors) has rank m − 1, from the singular value decomposition of Θ̂ = G Π̂ F′, G'G = Z̃'Z̃/n, F'F = (X̃'X̃/n)⁻¹ (their eqs. 11–14). With one endogenous regressor it is the Wald F of Π = 0. ivreg2's widstat.
  • effF (one endogenous regressor): the Montiel Olea & Pflueger (2013) effective F, π̂'Z̃'Z̃π̂ / tr(V̂(π̂) Z̃'Z̃); equal to kpF with one instrument.
  • coef, se: the first-stage coefficients of the instruments and their standard errors (instruments × endogenous) per horizon.

Two-way clustered variances are not repaired (psd is ignored, as in ivreg2): where the variance that a statistic needs is not positive definite, the statistic is NaN, with a warning.

PanelLocalProjections.synthetic — Function
synthetic(r, h; term = first shock, weights = :residual) -> NamedTuple

The synthetic time series of coefficient term at horizon h (Almuzara & Sancibrián 2026, Remark 1): per period of the sample, the weighted cross-sectional averages Σi wi zit / Σi wi² of the within-transformed regressor term (x), response (y) and residuals (resid), and the weight ωt = Σi wi² (weight, 0 for a period without weight, whose averages are NaN). The weights are those of the synthetic regression that the TLAHR refinement of term uses: the exposure of term for a shock (or another aggregate series times an exposure), of its shock for a lag of a shock, of the first shock otherwise, residualised per period (weights = :raw: the raw exposure, for x only). period holds the period labels (integers, or Dates with a frequency). A Tables.jl column table.
