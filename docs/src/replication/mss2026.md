# Mei, Sheng & Shi (2026)

*Nickell bias in panel local projection: Financial crises are worse than you think*,
Journal of International Economics, 104210.

The four applications use the authors' replication data. They are not redistributed with
the package; the authors publish them on
[GitHub](https://github.com/metricshilab/panel-lp-replication) and
[Mendeley](https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/p8sxs7sxjr/1). The test item
`MSS applications` in `test/test_mss_apps.jl` runs all of them when the data sit under
`docs/references/code/panel-lp-replication/applications/`.

## Specifications

Each horizon is a pre-built outcome column, estimated with `horizon = 0` as in the
authors' notebook. Singletons are kept, as in their code.

| application | outcome | shock | fixed effects | variance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Romer–Romer (2017), Fig. 4 | `f{h}LNGDP`, `f{h}UNEMP` | `CRISIS` | country + half-year, `OneSweep()` | `Clustered(:unit; ssc = :none)`, × 7 |
| Baron–Verner–Xiong (2021), Fig. 5 | `100 × Fd{h}y` | `R_B`, `R_N` | country | `Clustered(:unit, :time; ssc = :mss, psd = :raw)` |
| Mian–Sufi–Verner (2017), Fig. 6 | `F{h}y` | `L0HHD_L1GDP`, `L0NFD_L1GDP` | country | `Clustered(:unit, :time; ssc = :none, psd = false)` |
| Cerra–Saxena (2008), Fig. 7 | `cf{h}GRRT_WB` | `CRISIS` | country | `Clustered(:unit; ssc = :none)` |

```julia
r = panellp(@formula(Y ~ CRISIS + l1LNGDP + l2LNGDP + l3LNGDP + l4LNGDP +
                         l1CRISIS + l2CRISIS + l3CRISIS + l4CRISIS + fe(COUNTDUMS) + fe(halfyear)),
            data; id = :COUNTDUMS, time = :halfyear, horizon = 0,
            estimator = SplitPanelJackknife(), singletons = :keep, absorb = OneSweep())
```

## The one-sweep approximation

The authors' code demeans by country and then by half-year **once**. That is the exact
two-way within transformation only when the sample is balanced, which the GDP sample is.
There `OneSweep()` and exact alternating projections agree to rounding (about 1e-14). The
unemployment sample is unbalanced, and the published numbers depend on the approximation.
Exact two-way fixed effects give (× 7; standard errors clustered by country):

| ``h`` | FE, one sweep (published) | FE, exact | SPJ, one sweep (published) | SPJ, exact |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 2.049 (0.587) | 2.042 (0.585) | 2.321 (0.815) | 2.321 (0.715) |
| 7 | 2.128 (0.675) | 2.096 (0.672) | 2.632 (1.177) | 2.546 (0.960) |
| 10 | 1.345 (0.592) | 1.286 (0.597) | 1.660 (1.056) | 1.554 (0.928) |

Stata's `xtlp …, tfe`, whose two-way demeaning iterates, gives the exact columns too: to
1e-13 on the coefficients and 2e-10 on the standard errors, on both Romer–Romer samples
and at every horizon (a local test item).

The paper's conclusion is unchanged: SPJ responses exceed FE from ``h = 2`` on (at
``h = 0, 1`` they are slightly smaller, in both versions). But the peak SPJ response is
about 3% smaller, and the ``h = 10`` response about 6% smaller. The SPJ standard errors
depend on the approximation even more: with exact two-way fixed effects they are 12–18%
smaller, while the FE ones move by at most 1%.

## The split rule

The authors' code (like `xtlp`) gives the first ``\lceil n_i/2\rceil`` rows of a unit to
the first half. The paper defines the halves as ``t \le T_h/2``, which is
``\lfloor n_i/2\rfloor``. With an odd number of periods the two differ, and the Romer–Romer
GDP sample (balanced, ``T_h = 65`` at the Table 1 horizon) is affected.
`SplitPanelJackknife(split = :floor)`, the paper's rule, gives −6.606 at ``h = 7``
instead of the published −6.285, and −5.476 instead of −5.052 at ``h = 9`` (× 7).
